# Influencer Marketing Strategy — Dull is Dead / DiD

## 01 — Strategic Role of Influencer Marketing

For DiD, influencer marketing should not mean hiring lifestyle creators to promote an agency.

The right model is **creator and credibility partnerships**: founders, operators, marketing leaders, authors, podcasters, local ecosystem voices and niche experts who already speak to ambitious brands.

The goal is not mass awareness. The goal is to make the right brands feel seen, challenged and invited to apply.

DiD should use influencers to:

1. Build authority around anti-banality.
2. Reach founders and marketing decision-makers.
3. Create trust through third-party voices.
4. Drive qualified applications.
5. Support manual scouting with credible local signals.
6. Position DiD as selective, strategic and culturally sharp.

---

## 02 — Recommended Influencer Philosophy

DiD should not work with creators who make brands look busy.

DiD should work with people who can help brands think better.

The best partners are not the loudest voices. They are the ones with audience trust, strategic credibility and a clear point of view.

**Influencer marketing for DiD should feel like a curated intelligence network, not a paid shoutout campaign.**

---

## 03 — Influencer Tiers for DiD

### Nano influencers — 1K–10K followers

**Best for:** Local founders, niche operators, community leaders, small business mentors.

**Why they matter:** High trust, high authenticity, strong local relevance.

**Best use:** Local scouting, referrals, testimonials, LinkedIn comments, event amplification.

**Recommended role:** 50% of partnership activity.

### Micro influencers — 10K–100K followers

**Best for:** Marketing educators, founders with audience, LinkedIn creators, podcast hosts, business newsletter writers.

**Why they matter:** Strong reach and credibility without the cost of macro creators.

**Best use:** Thought leadership, co-authored posts, interviews, diagnostic content.

**Recommended role:** 40% of partnership activity.

### Macro voices — 100K+ followers

**Best for:** Selective launch moments, major credibility plays, large business audiences.

**Why they matter:** Reach and authority.

**Limitation:** Less personal, more expensive, harder to connect to qualified applications.

**Recommended role:** 10% of partnership activity.

### Mega / celebrity creators

**Recommendation:** Avoid.

They do not fit DiD’s selective, strategic and B2B positioning.

## 04 — Creator Categories to Target

### 1. Founder-operators

Founders who share lessons from building a brand.

**Why they fit:** DiD’s audience is made of founders and decision-makers. Founder-to-founder trust is powerful.

**Examples of profiles to look for:**
- Founders in food, wine, tourism, craft, B2B services and lifestyle.
- Founders with strong LinkedIn presence.
- Founders who talk about positioning, hiring, sales, brand or growth.

### 2. Marketing and branding educators

People who teach strategy, storytelling, SEO, content, design or growth.

**Why they fit:** They already educate the market. DiD can bring a sharper anti-banality angle.

**Examples of profiles to look for:**
- LinkedIn marketing creators.
- SEO/content strategists.
- Brand strategists.
- Copywriting educators.
- Design and web experts.

### 3. Local ecosystem voices

People with credibility in Vaucluse, Avignon, Carpentras, Orange, PACA and Rhône-Alpes.

**Why they fit:** DiD is locally rooted but strategically ambitious.

**Examples of profiles to look for:**
- Local business media.
- Chamber of commerce voices.
- Incubator mentors.
- Local founders with strong networks.
- Regional podcasters and newsletter writers.

### 4. Authors and intellectual voices

Camille’s book on brand storytelling can become a credibility asset.

**Why they fit:** DiD needs strategic authority, not only social reach.

**Examples of profiles to look for:**
- Business book authors.
- Storytelling experts.
- Brand strategy teachers.
- Editors and publishing voices.
- Podcast hosts interviewing founders.

### 5. Former clients and partners

Past clients, collaborators and ecosystem partners can become credibility amplifiers.

**Why they fit:** Their testimony can validate DiD’s model and working style.

**Important rule:** Never turn partners into scripted advocates. Use real experience, real results and real language.

## 05 — Platform Priority

### LinkedIn — Primary platform

LinkedIn is the best fit for DiD because the audience is B2B, strategic and decision-maker oriented.

**Best formats:**
- Diagnostic posts.
- Founder notes.
- Before/after message rewrites.
- Short case studies.
- Live conversations.
- Carousel frameworks.
- Commenting on founder posts.

### Instagram — Secondary platform

Instagram should support visual identity, manifesto energy and brand desirability.

**Best formats:**
- Anti-banality visuals.
- Reels with sharp one-line diagnostics.
- Behind-the-scenes strategy notes.
- Candidature reminders.
- Visual case studies.

### Podcasts and newsletters — High-value channels

These should be prioritized over generic influencer posts.

**Best formats:**
- Guest interviews.
- Co-authored essays.
- Newsletter features.
- Founder conversations.
- Strategic roundtables.

### TikTok / Reels — Experimental

Use only if the content can stay sharp and strategic.

Avoid trendy agency humor that weakens the brand.

## 06 — Campaign Concepts

### Campaign 1 — Anti-Banality Audit

**Objective:** Build authority and generate qualified applications.

**Creator angle:** Creators share their own banality score or audit a brand category.

**Content ideas:**
- “5 signs your brand is becoming interchangeable.”
- “Your product is strong. Your message is not.”
- “The cost of sounding like everyone else.”
- “Before/after: generic message vs. DiD message.”

**CTA:** `Votre marque peut postuler.`

### Campaign 2 — Founder Diagnostic Series

**Objective:** Attract founders through useful diagnosis.

**Format:** Short LinkedIn posts or videos where a creator interviews DiD about one brand problem.

**Episodes:**
- Why visibility is not the same as memorability.
- Why a logo cannot fix a weak strategy.
- Why cheap agencies are not the same as aligned pricing.
- Why brands need a baseline before a campaign.

### Campaign 3 — Local Pépites du Vaucluse

**Objective:** Support manual scouting and local credibility.

**Format:** DiD identifies promising local brands and shares public strategic observations, not unpaid audits.

**Tone:** Respectful, precise, non-flattering.

**Example angle:**
> On a repéré une marque locale avec un vrai potentiel. Le produit est fort. L’expression actuelle ne porte pas encore toute la différence.

**CTA:** Invite the brand privately, not publicly pressure it.

### Campaign 4 — Cheapmium Explained

**Objective:** Make the pricing model understandable and desirable.

**Creator angle:** Founder or marketing expert discusses why traditional agency pricing blocks ambitious brands.

**Key message:**
> Le fixe couvre le cadrage. La vraie rémunération vient de la valeur créée.

**CTA:** Learn if your brand can apply.

### Campaign 5 — Message Autopsy

**Objective:** Demonstrate DiD’s strategic edge.

**Format:** Anonymous before/after rewrites.

**Example:**
Before:
> Nous accompagnons les marques dans leur développement grâce à une approche créative et sur-mesure.

After:
> Votre marque vend peut-être déjà. Elle ne marque pas encore assez.

**CTA:** Follow DiD for sharper brand thinking.

### Campaign 6 — Camille, Storytelling Authority

**Objective:** Use Camille’s book and expertise to build credibility.

**Formats:**
- Podcast interviews.
- LinkedIn essays.
- Short video lessons.
- Newsletter guest posts.
- Live conversations with founders.

**Topics:**
- Why every brand has a story but few have a strategic one.
- How storytelling becomes a growth asset.
- The difference between a nice story and a usable brand narrative.

## 07 — Influencer Selection Criteria

Every potential partner should be scored before outreach.

| Criteria | What DiD should look for |
|---|---|
| Audience fit | Founders, CMOs, marketing leads, ambitious operators |
| Trust | Comments are real, audience engages thoughtfully |
| Content quality | Clear thinking, no generic agency clichés |
| Brand fit | Understands strategy, brand, growth or entrepreneurship |
| Tone | Sharp, intelligent, direct, non-corporate |
| Geography | Strong local fit or relevant French B2B audience |
| Conflict check | No direct competitor partnerships |
| Disclosure discipline | Clear about paid partnerships |
| Creative freedom | Can express ideas in their own voice |

### Red flags

- Generic business motivation content.
- Overuse of “impact”, “disruptif”, “360°”, “game-changing”.
- Fake engagement.
- Audience mostly students or unrelated consumers.
- No clear point of view.
- Too polished to feel credible.
- Willing to say anything for payment.

— [Nom]  
Dull is Dead / DiD

### Outreach rules

- Personalize every message.
- Mention a specific post, episode, article or point of view.
- Do not pitch “collaboration” vaguely.
- Bring one sharp topic.
- Keep it short.
- Follow up once after 7 days.
- Do not pay for fake enthusiasm.

## 09 — Influencer Brief Template

**Campaign:** Anti-Banality Audit

**Brand:** Dull is Dead / DiD

**One-line description:** Agence de croissance de marque sélective pour les marques à fort potentiel qui refusent la banalité.

**Audience:** Founders, CEOs, marketing directors and brand owners who have traction but feel their brand does not yet express their ambition.

**Primary message:** La banalité n’est pas un détail esthétique. C’est un risque business.

**Content objective:** Generate awareness, trust and qualified applications.

**Deliverables:**
- 1 LinkedIn post or newsletter section.
- Optional: 1 short conversation / interview.
- Optional: 1 Reel or carousel based on DiD framework.

**Tone:**
- Direct.
- Intelligent.
- Strategic.
- Non-corporate.
- No generic agency language.

**Phrases to use:**
- Le potentiel refuse la banalité.
- Votre marque peut postuler.
- La banalité coûte plus cher qu’un repositionnement.
- Le fixe est bas. L’exigence ne l’est pas.
- On ne vend pas de prestation. On choisit des marques.

**Phrases to avoid:**
- Agence 360°.
- Solution clé en main.
- Accompagnement global.
- Booster votre visibilité.
- Impactant.
- Disruptif.
- Game-changing.

**Creative freedom:** The creator should speak in their own voice. DiD does not want scripted praise. It wants a genuine strategic conversation.

**Disclosure:** Any paid partnership must be clearly disclosed with #sponsored or “Partenariat rémunéré” in the first line.

**Tracking:** Unique UTM link and application form source.

## 10 — Compensation Model

### Preferred model

For most partners, use a hybrid model:

- Fixed fee for content or appearance.
- Optional performance bonus for qualified applications.
- No payment per unqualified application.

### Suggested ranges

| Partner type | Suggested model |
|---|---|
| Nano local creator | Gift, invitation, small fee or referral bonus |
| Micro LinkedIn creator | €300–€1,500 per post or conversation |
| Newsletter writer | €500–€2,500 per feature |
| Podcast host | Guest appearance + optional paid sponsorship |
| Expert co-author | Revenue share, fee or cross-promotion |
| Macro business voice | €2,000–€10,000+ depending on reach and fit |

### Referral incentives

DiD can offer referral fees to trusted ecosystem partners for qualified introductions, not for raw applications.

A qualified referral should meet:

- Real ambition.
- Clear traction.
- Budget authority.
- Product/service quality.
- Willingness to be challenged.
- Fit with Cheapmium model.

## 11 — Tracking and Measurement

### Tracking tools

- UTM links per creator.
- Dedicated landing page: `/postuler?source=[creator]`
- Application form source field.
- CRM or Airtable scoring.
- Creator-specific discount/referral code if relevant.
- Post insights screenshots 48 hours after publication.

### Metrics by goal

| Goal | Primary metric | Secondary metric |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Reach, impressions, profile visits | Saves, shares, comments |
| Trust | Comment quality, replies, DMs | Newsletter signups |
| Applications | Qualified applications | Application conversion rate |
| Pipeline | Discovery calls booked | Pipeline value |
| Authority | Podcast/newsletter features | Backlinks, mentions |
| Referral | Qualified intros | Accepted projects |

### What matters most

- Number of qualified applications.
- Number of discovery calls.
- Quality of conversations.
- Selection rate.
- Pipeline value.
- Cost per qualified application.
- Cost per accepted project.

---

## 12 — Compliance

DiD must be clear and compliant.

### Disclosure rules

- Paid posts must disclose sponsorship clearly.
- Gifted collaborations should disclose the nature of the exchange.
- Affiliate or referral relationships must be disclosed.
- Do not ask creators to hide payment.
- Keep records of paid partnerships.

### Testimonial rules

- Never script a fake testimonial.
- Use real results only.
- Get written approval for client names, numbers and claims.
- Avoid promising growth outcomes through creator content.

## 13 — 90-Day Action Plan

### Days 1–30 — Foundation

- Build a list of 50 potential creators and ecosystem voices.
- Score them using the DiD criteria.
- Identify 10 high-priority partners.
- Prepare outreach messages.
- Create the Anti-Banality Audit landing page.
- Prepare 3 diagnostic content angles.

### Days 31–60 — First partnerships

- Secure 3–5 creator conversations.
- Publish 2 co-authored LinkedIn posts.
- Pitch 5 podcasts or newsletters.
- Launch one Local Pépites du Vaucluse observation series.
- Track applications by source.

### Days 61–90 — Optimization

- Double down on partners generating qualified conversations.
- Turn best posts into email newsletters.
- Create one case-style diagnostic post.
- Test one paid LinkedIn creator partnership.
- Review cost per qualified application.
- Build a round 2 partner list.

---

## 14 — Strategic Recommendation

DiD should not use influencer marketing like a consumer brand.

The right strategy is a **selective creator intelligence network**:

- LinkedIn first.
- Micro and nano voices first.
- Podcasts and newsletters over generic posts.
- Local Vaucluse credibility plus national strategic authority.
- No mega influencers.
- No scripted praise.
- No generic business motivation content.

The core creator message should be simple:

**Les marques à fort potentiel ne devraient pas mourir dans une communication banale. DiD les repère, les challenge et les aide à grandir.**
